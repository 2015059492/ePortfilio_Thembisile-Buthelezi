01 / Introduction

Designing learning that matters.

My practice sits at the intersection of Life Sciences education, educational technology, teacher development and digital equity. As a Fulbright scholar pursuing the M.Ed. in Educational Technology at the University of Delaware, I have explored how research-informed design can make education more accessible, culturally responsive and engaging. This portfolio documents six master's performances through the actual projects, research and designs created throughout my programme.

Discovering Plants / EDUC 485 Multimedia

An interactive journey through South African Life Sciences.

I created a navigable multimedia learning module exploring biodiversity, plant adaptations, indigenous medicinal plants, nutrition and conservation. It connects scientific concepts with Indigenous Knowledge Systems through clickable pathways, visual content, reflective prompts and a knowledge-check quiz.

Professional reflection

This project helped me translate multimedia learning theories into an interactive, culturally responsive learning resource. It reinforced the value of accessible navigation, meaningful visuals and learner-directed exploration rather than using multimedia simply for decoration.

Standards evidence to review

Design of learning experiences, digital content creation and equitable access. Final ISTE/AECT performance alignment should reference the specific standards selected for submission.

Systematic Literature Review / EDUC 485

Establishing the need for multimedia to support Indigenous Knowledge Systems.

My 20-page literature review (2000–2026) examined barriers to integrating IKS within South African Life Sciences, including teacher preparedness, limited instructional materials and challenges in implementing curriculum expectations. It draws on constructivism, situated cognition, metacognition, Cognitive Load Theory and Mayer's multimedia learning principles.

Professional reflection

The review strengthened my ability to begin educational technology design with an identified instructional need, rather than a tool. It provided a research-based rationale for creating culturally responsive multimedia resources, including my related Discovering Plants project.

Standards evidence to review

Research-informed needs analysis, learner and context analysis, and selection of instructional approaches.

Grade 11 Life Sciences / Learning experience design

Immune System Learning Lab.

A web-based learning experience organized around five active learning stations: Explore; Game and Simulation; Build; Apply; and AI and Reflection. Learners engage with visual explanations, interactive resources, modelling, real-world contexts and opportunities to reflect on their understanding of immunity.

Professional reflection

Designing this project deepened my appreciation for coherent sequencing of instructional activities and offering learners several ways to encounter, apply and demonstrate scientific understanding. The website format also enabled me to consider intuitive navigation and independent exploration.

Standards evidence to review

Curriculum-aligned instructional design, active learning strategies and formative assessment. Add the related field-experience journal if required by the performance rubric.

EDUC 611 / 2026–2027

KwaNxusa High School Technology Plan.

I developed a strategic plan focused on equitable technology integration in a rural KwaZulu-Natal school's Grade 10–12 Science Department. It proposes Google Classroom, PhET simulations, virtual labs and digital resources alongside teacher development, privacy guidance, accessibility measures, budgeting, staffing and implementation timelines.

Professional reflection

This planning exercise required me to consider technology integration as a whole-school implementation challenge. Sustainable improvement depends not just on procuring devices but also on teacher confidence, maintenance, leadership, accessibility and long-term resource constraints.

Standards evidence to review

Strategic leadership, equitable access, professional learning, safe use of digital resources and evaluation planning.

Digital equity / Scholarly presentation

What is the Digital Design Divide?

My research poster investigates implications of the digital design divide for teacher professional development in South Africa. It considers the capacity of educators to design meaningful, inclusive technology-enhanced learning — looking beyond access to devices alone — and presents literature-based findings, recommendations and future research directions.

Professional reflection

This work sharpened my focus on digital equity as a matter of instructional design capacity, not solely infrastructure. It encouraged me to examine how professional development and institutional support influence educators' ability to create valuable learning opportunities with technology.

Standards evidence to review

Research and evaluation, professional learning needs and advocacy for equitable instructional practice. The poster documents a research investigation; any required local intervention and outcome evidence should be added separately.
